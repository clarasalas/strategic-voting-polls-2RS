## The final dataset

| File | One row per | |
|---|---|---|
| `final/polls.csv` | poll × scenario × candidate | 693 polls: 98 (2002), 107 (2007), 124 (2012), 148 (2017), 216 (2022) |
| `final/scenarios.csv` | scenario | 1,149 scenarios: whether each is the poll's ballot scenario, and who stands in for whom |
| `final/corrections.csv` | item of the two worklists | 313 items, 136 applied, with the value before and after, or why not applied |

`polls.csv` has the columns of the interim tables (below), plus:

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `party` | the label at the time of the poll: the poll's Wikipedia row (Juppé is UMP in 2014, LR from 2015), else `config/parties.csv` |
| `slot` | the final candidacy whose place this candidate fills: the same person, or the same party at the time, allowing for renamings (UMP → LR, FN → RN, DLR → DLF, EM → LREM, LV → EELV, LCR → NPA). Empty for unaffiliated candidates and for parties absent from the ballot |
| `ballot_scenario` | this scenario is the poll's ballot scenario |
| `date` | `fieldwork_end`, or `reported_date` where the source gives no fieldwork (2007, 2012: publication date) |
| `corrected` | the row was changed by the validation (see `corrections.csv`) |
| `checked_against` | `other source` (matched in france-politique or NSPPolls), `original document` (spot check), both, or empty |

**Ballot scenario.** A scenario is a ballot scenario when every final candidacy with at least 2% of the vote has its
slot filled. It may also test candidates who will not be on the ballot (Taubira in 2022, Bayrou and Jadot in 2017):
that is the poll voters saw. Per poll, the one chosen has the most slots filled by the same person, then the fewest
extra candidates. Polls with one: 49 of 98 in 2002 (from February 2002), 107 of 107 in 2007, 116 of 124 in 2012,
109 of 148 in 2017, 169 of 216 in 2022. Rules in `src/polls/matching.py`.

<details>
<summary><strong>Columns of the poll tables</strong><br>Pollsters, share status, rolling polls, and the extra columns</summary>

<br>

One row per poll × scenario × candidate, in `interim/polls_{wikipedia,france_politique,nsppolls}.csv` and in
`final/polls.csv`. Shares are in percent.

**Pollsters.** `institute_raw` is the name as written in the source; `institute` is the pollster at the time,
without partner names ("Ifop-Fiducial" → Ifop, "Cevipof Ipsos-Sopra Steria" → Ipsos); `institute_family` groups
renamings of one company (Sofres, TNS Sofres, Kantar Sofres, Kantar Public → "Kantar (Sofres)"; Louis Harris, LH2 →
"LH2 (Louis Harris)"; Atlas Político → AtlasIntel). Harris Interactive is a separate company from Louis Harris/LH2.
`poll_id` is built from `institute_raw`, so editing the mapping never changes ids.

**`share_status`.**

| Value | Meaning | Share |
|---|---|---|
| `value` | a number | the number |
| `not_tested` | candidate not in this scenario (`—`, `ns`) | null |
| `grouped_others` | tested but counted in "others" (france-politique `autres`) | null |
| `below_threshold` | `<0,5 %`, `<1 %` | null; bound in `share_upper_bound` |
| `missing` | blank cell | null |

The pseudo-candidate `__others__` is the aggregate "Autres" row of the Ifop tables on france-politique. On Wikipedia,
the "Autres (candidats)" column lists extra named candidates and becomes ordinary candidate rows.

**`rolling`** is true only where the source says so: the `rolling` field in NSPPolls and "(rolling)" after the
pollster's name on the 2022 Wikipedia page. Otherwise it is null.

**Other columns.** `scenario_label` (NSPPolls hypothesis), `reported_date` (a single date when the source doesn't
say whether it is the end of fieldwork or the publication date), `date_raw`, `share_upper_bound`, `candidate_column`
(the source column, when the candidate is named inside the cell), `table_label`, and `source_poll_ref` (location in
the source, or the NSPPolls poll id).

<details>
<summary><strong>Limits and known issues</strong><br>What the data cannot do, and three small open points</summary>

<br>

**Limits.** 2007 and 2012 dates are publication dates, usually 1 to 5 days after fieldwork. Small candidates, mostly
at 2% or less, are missing in many 2007 and 2012 polls, so shares can sum below 100; they were not added, not even
for the polls checked by hand, so as not to mix two standards in one table. Before a party chose its candidate, the
share can be filed under the eventual candidate (Mamère for Voynet or Lipietz in 2002). Sample sizes are given only
from 2017, and are the base of respondents giving a voting intention, about 91–95% of the full sample.

**Open points**, small and left as they are:

* 32 of the 1,862 parsed scenarios have shares summing outside 98–102: 19 on the 2012 Wikipedia page, 4 in NSPPolls
  (some summing to 0). Listed at the end of notebook 01.
* One parse error: a 2017 Wikipedia cell reading `640-646` (`interim/parse_errors.csv`).
* The results file is pinned to the commit of a merged pull request of strategic-voting-abm-2RS
  ([#24](https://github.com/clarasalas/strategic-voting-abm-2RS/pull/24)). To re-pin to `main`, blank
  `results.commit` in `config/sources.yaml` and rerun notebook 01.

</details>

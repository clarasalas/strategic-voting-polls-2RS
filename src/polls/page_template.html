French presidential elections · 2002–2022

What the polls said before the first round

Every first-round poll of five presidential campaigns: how the race looked, who seemed headed for the runoff, and how the vote moved in the last weeks.

Polls are not what voters expect, and a result is not the polls' target: it also holds the last-minute switching the polls could not see. A gap between them mixes poll error and real movement, which these data cannot separate. Five elections, described rather than explained.

2 · Who reaches the runoff

The pair is clear months ahead, unless the 2nd and 3rd are close

In 2007, 2012 and 2022 every poll of the last two weeks named the real runoff pair. In 2002 none did, and in 2017 the pair settled only three months out.

What decides the runoff is the gap between the 2nd and the 3rd. When it is wide, the polls name the pair months ahead; when it is a point or two, as in 2002, 2017 and 2022, a small error is enough to get it wrong.

3 · The last weeks

Near the threshold, the result tilts toward the 3rd

The candidate 3rd in the last two weeks of polls did at least as well as polled in all five elections, by 2.6 points on average. The 4th did worse in four of five.

That is the direction strategic voting would push, toward a candidate within reach of the runoff and away from one just out of it. A poll bias could produce the same pattern: the two cannot be told apart here.

4 · Concentration

The vote gathers at the end, not over the campaign

Between the last polls and the result, the vote concentrated in all five elections. Over the campaign, the polls mostly spread out.

Concentration is the Herfindahl–Hirschman index (HHI), the sum of the squared shares; its inverse is the effective number of candidates. 2002 stands apart: almost nine effective candidates in the result, against about five in the other four.

Where the change in HHI comes from

Voters: shares moving among the candidates who will win at least 2%, a fixed set. Offer: candidates tested in the polls and later withdrawing. Campaign: from the opening polls to the last two weeks; last mile: from the last two weeks to the result.

5 · Pollsters and momentum

Precise polls, small house effects, movement that carries on

Single polls scatter about as much as random samples of 900 to 1,800 people would. Pollsters differ from each other by half a point on a typical candidate, at most about two.

A candidate rising in the polls tends to keep rising, by about 0.4 times as much over the next three weeks (95% interval 0.29–0.53), and the trend of the last month carries on into the result.

The data. The polls come from the French Wikipedia lists of polls, at fixed revisions, checked share by share against france-politique.fr (2002) and NSPPolls (2022), and against the pollsters' reports and the Commission des sondages notices: 693 polls, 136 corrections. Each poll is read through its scenario with the full ballot, shares rescaled to 100. Rolling polls are thinned so that each interview counts once.

The poll average weights polls by their distance in days (Gaussian kernel, 10 days); where polls are few, the window widens and the line is pale. Every statistic on this page uses the fixed window. Dates are the end of fieldwork, the publication date in 2007 and 2012.
